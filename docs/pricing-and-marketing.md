# Araiza Assembly: internal pricing and first-five-customer plan

This document is internal quoting and marketing guidance for the prototype. It is not customer-facing copy, and the ranges are starting points rather than promises.

## Pricing position

Target roughly **$45-$65 per hour of productive assembly time**, then quote a flat customer price whenever the item and scope can be understood in advance.

Keep the public offer simple:

- Free quotes from a photo, product link, model number, or description.
- Flat-rate pricing whenever the scope is clear.
- $50 minimum service call.
- Any scope change is confirmed before continuing.

The site shows only a few public starting points. Keep the full table below internal so Araiza Assembly can account for access, complexity, travel, and bundling without boxing itself into a rigid menu.

## Simple quoting formula

When the assembly time is uncertain:

```text
labor base = estimated productive hours x $45-$65
complexity allowance = labor base x 0%-25%
scope additions = mounting + two-person handling + travel + disposal/cleanup
customer quote = max($50, labor base + complexity allowance + scope additions)
```

Use the lower end when the item is straightforward, access is easy, and instructions are clear. Use the upper end when there are many pieces, drawers or doors, awkward weight, mounting, tight stairs, unclear instructions, or a realistic chance the product needs extra troubleshooting.

### Adjustment guide

- **Number of pieces:** add time for sorting, labeling, and setup, not only final assembly.
- **Drawers and doors:** add roughly 5%-15% for alignment and repeated hardware work.
- **Mounting or securing:** add roughly $15-$40+ depending on wall type, hardware, and whether a second person is needed. Quote mounting separately when uncertain.
- **Size and weight:** add roughly $25-$60+ when safe handling needs a second person or more setup space.
- **Travel:** keep the $50 minimum inside the core area; add a clear travel amount for longer drives rather than hiding it in the labor rate.
- **Multiple-person jobs:** price the extra productive labor and state when two-person handling is required.
- **Disposal and cleanup:** add roughly $15-$40 when customers want packaging broken down or removed. Confirm disposal limits before promising removal.
- **Complex instructions:** use a complexity allowance instead of guessing an optimistic time. Ask for the manual or product link when available.
- **Bundles:** for two or more items in one visit, reduce repeated setup time by about 5%-10% while keeping the total quote above the minimum and the target hourly rate.

## Quote intake checklist

Before giving a flat price, collect:

1. Product link, model number, or clear photos of the box/manual.
2. Item count and whether all boxes and hardware are present.
3. City or location and any long-distance travel concern.
4. Room, stairs, narrow access, or need to move an existing piece.
5. Drawers, doors, lift mechanisms, glass, or unusually heavy parts.
6. Mounting or anti-tip securing requirements.
7. Packaging breakdown or disposal expectation.
8. Whether multiple items can be assembled during the same visit.

## Introductory offer

Use one offer consistently:

> **New-customer bundle:** $10 off a visit of $75 or more when two or more furniture items are quoted and assembled during the same visit. The $50 minimum still applies.

This encourages customers to send the full list without making the business look like a clearance service. Do not stack it with another discount, and do not discount a job that already prices below the target productive rate.

## First five paying customers: 14-day plan

The goal is five real paid jobs, not five likes. Track every inquiry in a simple sheet with source, item, city, quoted amount, booked date, outcome, and follow-up date.

### Suggested $100-$200 allocation

- $0: Google Business Profile, Facebook groups, Marketplace where permitted, Nextdoor, outreach calls.
- $20-$45: 50-100 simple flyers or service cards.
- $20-$40: basic magnetic car signs or a small yard sign only if allowed.
- $10-$25: one local-classified test or QR-code printing.
- $50-$90: reserve for a second print run, a small local boost, or marketplace fees only after an organic post proves it gets inquiries.

Do not spend the full budget on ads before the quote process has been tested. The first goal is learning which furniture types and neighborhoods respond.

## Reviews and referrals

Ask for a Google review only after the job is complete and the customer has had a chance to inspect the work. Do not filter for positive customers, offer a reward for a review, or write a review for anyone.

Suggested message:

> Thanks for having Araiza Assembly handle the setup today. If you are comfortable sharing an honest Google review, it would help a new local business a lot. Here is the direct link: [Google review link]. No pressure either way.

For referrals, say:

> If someone in your building or neighborhood needs furniture assembled, feel free to share our number. The new customer can mention your name, and we can apply the $10 referral credit to your next $75+ visit.

Only apply a referral credit after the referred customer completes a paid visit. Keep the $50 minimum and write the terms on the quote or receipt.

## Turn one job into the next job

- Ask for the complete furniture list before quoting. Beds, nightstands, dressers, desks, and shelves often arrive together.
- Offer a same-visit bundle whenever the items can be handled safely and the customer can clear the work area.
- Ask permission to follow up after 30 days with a short message about any remaining furniture from the move.
- Give apartment residents a simple card they can pass to a neighbor. Avoid leaving materials where the property does not permit them.
- Ask movers, mattress stores, and storage facilities to pass along the photo-first quote process rather than promising an unapproved partnership.
- Save the customer's preferred contact method and item type with permission so future follow-up is useful, not generic.

## What to measure

After 14 days, review:

- Number of inquiries by channel.
- Quote-to-booking rate.
- Average quoted amount and actual productive hours.
- Most common furniture types.
- Travel time and unproductive time.
- Number of customers who sent a photo or product link.
- Reviews requested and reviews received, without review gating.

Keep the channel that produces paid work, not the channel with the most views.
